# TerraRisk Water Intelligence × WELL Labs — Alignment Report

**Date:** 31 July 2026
**Purpose:** Validate — not assume — that TerraRisk's scientific outputs and decision-support logic are consistent with WELL Labs' own publicly documented approach to water in Raichur, using only public evidence.
**Evidence tiers:** **[S]** = sourced (linked). **[E]** = estimate/inference, explicitly labelled. Wherever public evidence is insufficient, this document says so directly rather than guessing.

---

## Executive summary

WELL Labs has a real, named, public, satellite-based methodology directly relevant to this comparison: **Jaltol** — a free, open-source QGIS plugin (also released as a web tool), co-developed by WELL Labs with ATREE's Centre for Social and Environmental Innovation (CSEI), built on **Google Earth Engine**, combining rainfall, evapotranspiration, soil moisture, surface-water/groundwater storage change, and land-use/land-cover (via IIT Delhi's IndiaSAT dataset) into one water-accounting platform, explicitly used for **watershed-intervention before/after impact evaluation** (documented case studies in Rajasthan, Jharkhand, Marathwada) **[S: [Jaltol — QGIS plugin repository](https://plugins.qgis.org/plugins/jaltol-main/), [Jaltol & Water Security](https://welllabs.org/jaltol-water-security-digital-tools-data-evapotranspiration-how-do-you-track-water-usage/), [Evaluating Watershed Interventions Using Jaltol](https://welllabs.org/mel-toolbox-part-4/)]**. Critically, WELL Labs' own materials confirm the **Jaltol framework is what underlies their Raichur village water accounts work** with ACIWRM/IHE Delft **[S: [Remote Sensing for Communities](https://welllabs.org/well-labs-partnership-ihe-delft-aciwrm-water-remote-sensing-data/)]** — this is not a generic other-state tool, it is the real methodological basis for WELL Labs' own Raichur remote-sensing work.

This changes the comparison from "no evidence exists" to a genuine, evidence-grounded methodological comparison. The finding, stated plainly: **TerraRisk and Jaltol are built on the same core inputs (rainfall, ET, surface water, LULC, Google Earth Engine) and address adjacent problems (water accounting, intervention impact evaluation) using different, non-identical methods and different emphases** (Jaltol centres on crop-water-requirement/water-budgeting for community planning and MEL; TerraRisk centres on a catchment mass-balance and a composite recharge-stress score for portfolio triage). **Nowhere in public evidence does WELL Labs publish the specific equation, weighting, or thresholds either tool uses** — so precise numerical agreement cannot be validated, and this report does not claim it. What can be validated is directional and structural consistency, which is documented below, output by output.

**Which outputs closely align:** field-validation logic (matches WELL Labs' own documented practice almost exactly in spirit), trend/comparison analysis (matches Jaltol's stated purpose and the drone survey's own precedent).
**Which outputs differ:** the water-balance framing itself (TerraRisk = satellite mass balance; WELL Labs' flagship published report = survey-based urban accounting); the vegetation output (stress index vs. validated cropping classification).
**Which outputs are unsupported by public WELL Labs evidence either way:** the prioritisation/ranking logic — no comparable published WELL Labs ranking exists to check TerraRisk's Priority Queue against.

## Part C — Answers to the seven questions

**1. If a WELL Labs hydrologist looked at TerraRisk, would they broadly agree with our interpretation?**
Likely broad, qualified agreement, not full endorsement. The core inputs (rainfall, ET, surface water, LULC, GEE) and several outputs (trend detection, field-validation instinct) are the same family WELL Labs' own Jaltol uses **[S]**. But a WELL Labs hydrologist would very likely flag two things: (a) TerraRisk's composite 0–100 stress score has no published WELL Labs analogue to check it against, and (b) TerraRisk's surface-water percentile is exactly the input WELL Labs' own team has publicly said needs field supplementation because remote sensing alone isn't reliable enough there **[S]** — so agreement on inputs, real caution warranted on the surface-water output specifically.

**2. Would our recommendation for a Raichur village likely agree with what WELL Labs' current workflow would recommend?**
**Cannot fully validate from public information** — no published WELL Labs output gives a specific village's recommendation to compare against. What can be said: WELL Labs' own workshop composition (deliberately mixing head-end/tail-end/dryland farmers) and the drone survey's tail-end finding **[S]** suggest that for a *canal-command* village, TerraRisk's Compare workflow (head vs. tail catchments) would likely surface the same *direction* of finding (tail-end worse off) that WELL Labs has already found by other means — but the exact magnitude and the specific action WELL Labs' own field team would take cannot be checked publicly.

**3. Which recommendations are scientifically sound?**
"Needs field validation" (low confidence/completeness) and "declining trend" (3 consecutive worsening runs) are the most defensible — they encode caution and require sustained evidence rather than a single noisy reading, consistent with WELL Labs' own field-validation and MEL practices **[S]**.

**4. Which recommendations need modification?**
"Needs a field visit" driven by the surface-water factor specifically should carry a stronger caveat — WELL Labs' own team has flagged this exact satellite signal as needing field supplementation **[S]**, and TerraRisk currently presents it with the same confidence framing as the other two factors.

**5. Which outputs should be removed because they are unsupported?**
None should be *removed* — but the composite 0–100 "stress score" should never be presented as validated against WELL Labs' own methodology, since no comparable published WELL Labs score exists to validate it against. It should be presented as TerraRisk's own composite, not a WELL Labs-equivalent measure.

**6. Which outputs should be renamed because WELL Labs uses different terminology?**
"Water balance" is the one term worth being careful with in a WELL Labs conversation specifically — WELL Labs' most public use of that exact phrase (Bengaluru/Chintamani) means a survey-based urban accounting exercise, not a satellite catchment mass balance **[S]**. Using the same words for a different method risks a founder assuming more overlap than exists. No change to the product's own UI is proposed here (Part 8 forbids it) — this is a note for how to *talk about* it in the demo.

**7. Can TerraRisk be presented as a complementary decision-support tool, or a direct replacement?**
**Complementary — clearly, on the public evidence.** WELL Labs' own ground network covers roughly 1% of its stated target area **[S]**; TerraRisk's satellite coverage requires no field crew and can reach anywhere with a catchment defined. Nothing in public evidence supports claiming TerraRisk replaces the community hydrologist network, the drone survey, or Jaltol — each does something TerraRisk does not (ground truth, cm-resolution defect detection, crop-water-requirement budgeting, respectively). TerraRisk's honest position is: the wide-area, always-on, no-field-crew-required screening layer that flags where WELL Labs' own more precise (but scarcer) methods should be pointed next.

## Part D — Alignment Report: overall score

| Dimension | Score | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Scientific alignment** | 65% | Same core physical inputs as Jaltol (rainfall, ET, surface water, LULC, GEE) **[S]** — real, evidenced overlap. Docked for the composite stress score and storage-change bands having no public WELL Labs equivalent to check against, and for conflating "water balance" with WELL Labs' differently-methodled flagship report if not careful. |
| **Hydrological alignment** | 60% | The P−ET−Q=dS mass-balance shape is a standard, defensible approach and shares its input family with Jaltol's water-balance estimation **[S]**. Docked because WELL Labs' own published water-balance work (Bengaluru/Chintamani) is not this method, and no public Jaltol equation was found to confirm numerical (not just input-family) alignment. |
| **Decision-support alignment** | 55% | Directionally right instinct (field-validation, trend, comparison) mirrors WELL Labs' own documented practices closely in spirit **[S]**. Docked heavily because no published WELL Labs ranking/prioritisation output exists to validate Priority Queue's severity ordering against — this dimension is the least publicly checkable of the five. |
| **GIS workflow alignment** | 50% | Both operate at village/catchment granularity with satellite+GEE tooling **[S]**. Docked substantially: WELL Labs' drone survey resolves individual canal defects at cm-scale **[S]**, and Jaltol integrates 10m IndiaSAT LULC specifically; TerraRisk's GIS ceiling today is an administrative-boundary polygon with no canal-command layer, no DEM/slope analysis, and no bulk village-water-account generation. |
| **Field-validation alignment** | 70% | The highest score, and the most directly evidenced: WELL Labs' own validated satellite classification (QField, 10 enumerators, published accuracy figures) **[S]** is the exact discipline TerraRisk's "needs validation" recommendation is trying to encode. Not 100%, because TerraRisk's own outputs have never actually been field-checked the way that classification was — the *intent* aligns strongly, the *execution* has not yet been proven the same way. |

**Overall alignment score: 60%**
(Simple average of the five dimensions above, each independently justified and cited.)

**What this number means, and does not mean:** 60% reflects a real, evidenced, directionally-consistent relationship between TerraRisk and WELL Labs' own public methodology — not a validated reproduction of it, and not a rejection of it either. The honest, defensible claim, and the only one this report supports: **TerraRisk reaches broadly similar kinds of conclusions to WELL Labs' own tools, using overlapping inputs and a different specific method, and should be introduced to a WELL Labs founder in exactly those terms.**
